## Key Question 1: Step-by-step finishing workflow (raw plywood to packed product) and where scratch resistance is built in

### Takeaway
No single published source documents a complete raw-plywood-to-box workflow for a touring-grade factory; the picture has to be assembled from fragments: void-free birch or marine plywood with D4 glue, CNC/assembly with fast-set glue under press, a two-pass sprayed texture (base coat + spatter coat, ~120 µm total for water-based acrylic texture paint) or a sprayed plural-component polyurea/polyurethane "hard-coat" (typically 20–40 mils / 0.5–1 mm), then functional sweep/impulse test, visual inspection, packaging. Scratch/abrasion resistance is built in principally by (a) the coating chemistry and thickness (polyurea PCP vs. ~0.12 mm acrylic texture) and (b) the sealed, exterior-grade substrate; no maker publishes edge-radius or sanding-grit data.

### Inferences
- Two distinct factory finishing architectures are visible: (1) water-based 1K acrylic/urethane texture paint sprayed in two passes to ~120 µm (Warnex/Armacab/DuraTex class; low capex, 2–3 h to handle, 1 day to pack, 4 days full cure), and (2) plural-component polyurea/PU "hard-coat" (d&b PCP, KV2 PCP, Penn Guard, Line-X class; high capex heated plural-component rig, seconds to tack-free, 0.5–1 mm films). Weatherized and flagship touring lines cluster on (2); install/contractor lines and smaller brands on (1).
- Scratch resistance is therefore primarily a chemistry + film-build decision made at the coating-selection stage, secondarily a substrate decision (void-free/marine birch with D4 glue so the coating is not undermined by swelling), and only tertiarily a geometry decision (recessed handles, rounded edges) for which no manufacturer publishes numbers.
- L-Acoustics' model (box build + paint at a captive sister plant, powder coating at an acquired metal plant, final assembly at HQ) suggests large makers treat cabinet finishing as a separable, vertically integrated upstream operation rather than an in-line step at the assembly site.

### Gaps
- No manufacturer publishes a sanding sequence (grit progression), void-filling practice, masking method, or sealer/primer product for touring cabinets; all such detail available online is DIY/forum.
- No maker publishes coat counts or DFT for its production finish (only the Warnex coating-maker TDS and generic polyurea ranges).
- No independent factory-tour article describing the spray line itself at d&b, L-Acoustics/Simea, Meyer, Adamson, Martin Audio, Funktion-One, Void, EAW or RCF was retrievable; the d&b and L-Acoustics tours mention gluing/pressing and testing but not painting. Adamson Port Perry search returned nothing relevant.
- JBL's "DuraFlex" chemistry is not described in any retrieved source.

## Key Question 3: How the finish is cured and how long before handling/boxing

### Takeaway
For water-based texture paint, the coating maker (Warnex) specifies room-temperature dust-dry in 15–20 min, handling/assembly in 2–3 h, packaging in 1 day, full cure in 4 days, with optional 60 °C forced drying; for polyurea PCP-class finishes, tack-free is seconds (Penn Guard 4–12 s) with full cure in roughly 10–24 h. No loudspeaker manufacturer publishes its own oven/booth schedule.

### Cited Findings
- Warnex: 2 spray passes ≥15–20 min apart; dust-dry 15–20 min; assembly-safe 2–3 h; packaging-safe 1 day; through-dried 4 days (room temperature); heat acceleration at 60 °C possible; dependent on air/object temperature and film thickness **[CONFIRMED – coating maker]** — [Warnex Datenblatt](https://images.thomann.de/pics/prod/208234_datenblatt.pdf)
- Penn Guard polyurea: cure 4–12 seconds **[CONFIRMED – supplier]** — [LSi Online](https://lsionline.com/news/penn-guard-now-in-smaller-packages-n581k0)
- Generic polyurea: dry to touch 3 s–20 min; complete cure 10–24 h at 75 °F **[GENERIC]** — [ArmorThane polyurea guide](https://armorthane.com/polyurea-guide/)
- Polyurea is sensitive to ambient moisture during cure and needs precise gun setup and environmental control **[LOW-QUALITY buying guide]** — [Alibaba wooden speaker box paint guide 2026](https://electronics.alibaba.com/buyingguides/wooden-speaker-box-paint-guide-what-works-in-2026)
- Focal (hi-fi): hand spray followed by curing time; booths run to a schedule **[GENERIC]** — [SoundStage Australia](https://soundstageaustralia.com/index.php/features/372-factory-tour-focal-part-1-respect-for-wood)
- Hi-fi cabinet maker: parts cured "in a climate-controlled room" **[MARKETING]** — [Woodsat](https://woodsat.com/speaker-box-finishes/)
- d&b assembly uses fast-setting glue with press (cabinet gluing, not coating cure) **[CONFIRMED – d&b]** — [CX Network](https://cxnetwork.com.au/?p=12079)

### Inferences
- A texture-paint line that boxes after 1 day and ships within 4 days is shipping cabinets that are not fully through-cured; this is consistent with the Warnex "packaging-safe" vs "through-dried" distinction and argues for protective packaging/film at pack-out.
- The seconds-to-tack-free behaviour of polyurea is the throughput driver that justifies its equipment cost (Penn-Elcom's own "boosts productivity" framing).

### Gaps
- No pro-audio maker's booth temperature, IR/forced-air use, or dwell time before hardware install is published.

## Key Question 4: Which test standards speaker makers cite for finish durability and what pass criteria they use

### Takeaway
Loudspeaker makers publicly cite environmental/corrosion standards (ISO 9227 / ASTM B117 salt spray, ISO 4892-2 / ISO 11341 UV, MIL-STD-810G/H, SAE J2334, IEC 60529 IP codes, EN 204 D4 glue) with a few numeric criteria (L-Acoustics: 48 h 5% salt spray, 10 days UV at 800 W/50 °C with "only very slight color deviation", 5-day 95% RH 30/60 °C humid cycle; Martin Audio CDD Marine: 1600 h UV ISO 4892-2 method A cycle B; Atlas: 100 h ASTM B117). No loudspeaker maker was found publishing Taber (ASTM D4060/ISO 7784), pencil-hardness (D3363), cross-hatch (D3359), scratch (D7027) or impact (D2794) results or pass thresholds for cabinet finishes.

### Cited Findings

**Manufacturer-cited standards and criteria**

## Key Question 8 (objective items): Regulatory constraints, automation trends, cost/throughput

### Takeaway
EU REACH restriction (Reg. 2020/1149) made training mandatory from 24 Aug 2023 for industrial/professional users of products with >0.1% monomeric diisocyanates (renew every 5 years); UK HSE enforces clearance-time, airline BA and urine biological monitoring for isocyanate sprayers; OSHA's MDI ceiling is 0.02 ppm; EU Paints Directive 2004/42/EC caps two-pack reactive coatings at 140 g/l (WB) / 500 g/l (SB) and SCAQMD Rule 1136 governs wood-product coatings in Southern California. Cost data is thin: water-based texture ≈ <$0.75/sq ft materials vs forum-quoted $150–200/cabinet for shop-applied polyurea (2015); polyurea's seconds-fast cure is the main throughput argument.

### Inferences
- For an EU/UK factory, choosing polyurea/2K PU over 1K water-based texture paint triggers the REACH training regime, HSE-style booth clearance/airline BA/biological monitoring, and higher capex, offset by seconds-fast cure and higher film build; this trade-off, not coating performance alone, likely explains why install/contractor lines stay on water-based texture paint.
- The EU Paints Directive formally covers decorative/vehicle-refinish products; an industrial speaker-cabinet line's solvent emissions would more likely be governed by the Industrial Emissions Directive solvent chapter (not researched here — see gaps).
