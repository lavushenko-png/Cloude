## Key question 1: What is the chemistry difference between polyurea, polyurethane, polyaspartic and water-based acrylic texture coatings, and how does each affect scratch/chip resistance on wood cabinets?

### Takeaway
All of the "bed-liner" family are isocyanate-cured thermosets whose differences lie in the co-reactant (polyol → polyurethane, amine → polyurea, aspartic ester → polyaspartic) and in whether the isocyanate is aromatic (cheap, fast, yellows/chalks) or aliphatic (UV-stable). Water-based "speaker cabinet paints" (Warnex, Tuff Cab, Duratex) are 1K acrylate dispersions that dry by coalescence; they are thinner, softer and far safer to apply, and rely on texture and easy touch-up rather than elastomeric toughness. Elastomeric spray liners applied at 1–3 mm resist scuffing and chipping because they absorb impact energy through elongation and sheer thickness, whereas hard thin films (2K PU, epoxy, powder) resist scratching by hardness but crack or chip on a flexing wood substrate.

### Cited Findings
**Reactant chemistry**
- Polyurethane coatings form from an isocyanate reacting with a polyol; aromatic hardeners yellow while aliphatic hardeners are light-stable; catalysts are needed and cure is slow (one source cites 24–48 h) — [Duraamen, "What is the difference between Polyaspartic and Polyurethane?"](https://duraamen.com/blog/what-is-the-difference-between-polyaspartic-and-polyurethane-sealers/)
- Polyurea replaces the polyol with a multifunctional amine; the amine–isocyanate reaction is self-accelerating, needs no catalyst and gels in seconds — [ArmorThane, "What are the major differences between polyurea and polyurethane"](https://www.armorthane.com/resources/news/what-are-the-major-differences-between-polyurea-and-polyurethane/); [SpecialChem polyurea guide](https://www.specialchem.com/adhesives/guide/polyurea-adhesives-and-sealants)
- Polyaspartic is a subset of polyurea: an aliphatic isocyanate reacting with an aspartic ester that behaves like a hindered diamine ("not all polyurea is polyaspartic, however all polyaspartic is polyurea"); its main practical gain is speed (cure in hours) and UV stability — [Xtreme Polishing Systems, polyurea vs polyaspartic](https://xtremepolishingsystems.com/blogs/decorative-concrete-and-epoxy-blog/polyurea-vs-polyaspartic-coatings); [Duraamen](https://duraamen.com/?p=6648)
- "Hybrid" polyurea/polyurethane systems mix urethane and urea linkages and "can display some of the negative problems associated with polyurethane chemistry" (moisture sensitivity, slower cure) — [ArmorThane](https://www.armorthane.com/resources/news/what-are-the-major-differences-between-polyurea-and-polyurethane/)
- One source claims polyaspartic cannot be formulated waterborne, while polyurethanes can — [Duraamen](https://duraamen.com/blog/what-is-the-difference-between-polyaspartic-and-polyurethane-sealers/)
- PCI Magazine overview article covering polyurethane and polyurea coating chemistry (could not be opened in this session) — [PCI Magazine, "Polyurethane and Polyurea Coatings"](https://pcimag.com/articles/112052-polyurethane-and-polyurea-coatings)

**Aromatic vs aliphatic / UV**
- "Aromatic coatings fade and aliphatic coatings do not. Most standard spray-on bedliner products are aromatic"; pigment is added before spraying and "air and time will cause the bedliner to fade and oxidize"; makers sell an aliphatic topcoat that "eliminates the risk fading even in prolonged and intense direct sunlight" — [BodyShop Business, "Tough and Durable Sprayed-On Bedliner"](https://www.bodyshopbusiness.com/tough-and-durable-sprayed-on-bedliner/); [ArmorThane bed liners](https://www.armorthane.com/protective-coating-applications/truck-bedliner-applications/bed-liners.htm); [ArmorThane, important things to know about polyurea](https://www.armorthane.com/important-things-to-know-about-polyurea-coatings)
- A clear aliphatic polyurea topcoat TDS states it is "chemically UV stable which allows it to protect and extend the life of other non-UV stable surfaces" — [VFI-2580 TDS, Volatile Free Inc.](https://volatilefree.com/vfi-2580-tds)
- Rhino Linings sells an aliphatic UV-stable topcoat ("Rhino Shine Ultra") for use over its liners — [Rhino Shine Ultra](https://liners.rhinolinings.com/en/products/rhino-shine-ultra)

**Moisture-cure 1K polyurethanes (roll-on liners)**

**Why the families behave differently on cabinets (community/OEM evidence)**
- "If you want something that will withstand many scuffs, nothing will outlast a two-component polyurea coating like Line-X, or equivalent"; another poster describes Line-X as "harder and less flexible and is very rough to the touch" — [DIYAudio, "Duratex or other options"](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/duratex-or-other-options.419572/)
- A Line-X-coated cabinet "is much thicker and more resilient than the Duratex that I applied at home" — [TalkBass, "Coating a bass cab in Line-X"](https://www.talkbass.com/threads/coating-a-bass-cab-in-line-x.1265520/)
- JBL's AE-series array guide warns against epoxy paints on its wooden cabinets because they can crack, and notes water-based paint adheres less well to the aliphatic top layer of DuraFlex — [JBL AC26 Array Guide p.30](https://www.manualowl.com/m/JBL/AC26/Manual/717474?page=30)
- Line-X 2015 explainer: Platinum 3065 psi tensile / 750 lbs/in tear / 203 % elongation vs Line-X Standard 2147 psi / 490 lbs/in / 91 % — illustrating that the "premium" liners trade some hardness for much higher tear strength and elongation — [Line-X, "Making a bedliner indestructible: it's science"](https://linex.com/news/2015/08/06/bedliner-indestructible)

### Inferences
- The scratch/chip resistance ranking on plywood/MDF follows thickness × toughness rather than hardness alone: a 1.5 mm Shore-D-55 polyurea (tear 400–750 pli, elongation 80–200 %) spreads a point load over a large volume and recovers elastically; a 0.1–0.2 mm acrylic film (Tuff Cab/Warnex/Duratex) has far less material to lose before the substrate shows, so these products rely on texture (which hides fine scratches and makes scuffs less visible) and on easy local re-coating.
- Hard, thin, highly cross-linked films (2K PU clearcoats, epoxies, powder coat) score well on pencil hardness and Taber loss but are brittle relative to wood movement; this is consistent with JBL's explicit warning against epoxy on wooden cabinets and with the general polyurethane/epoxy cracking behaviour described by coating vendors.
- Aromatic polyurea (the standard, cheapest hot-spray liner chemistry) is the right choice for black cabinets kept mostly indoors; any coloured or outdoor cabinet needs an aliphatic topcoat or an all-aliphatic system, which is why OEM "weather-resistant" variants (JBL WRC/WRX, d&b PCP outdoor) are specified separately.
- The glycol-ether content (6–9 wt%) of the Warnex SDS shows that "water-based" texture paints are low-VOC, not zero-VOC; they still need ventilation when sprayed.

### Gaps
- Could not open the PCI Magazine polyurethane/polyurea article or the ArmorThane explainer directly; typical gel-time numbers (seconds for polyurea vs minutes for hybrid) and the chemistry of Line-X's individual grades (which are aromatic polyurea, which hybrid, which aliphatic) are not confirmed from primary pages in this session.
- No source found in this session that quantitatively relates elastomer elongation/tear strength to chip resistance on wood (e.g., Gardner impact ASTM D2794 data for liners on plywood). This is an inference only.
- Binder details for Duratex (acrylic vs acrylic-urethane hybrid) and Tuff Cab (acrylic copolymer type) were not disclosed in the retrieved extracts.

## Key question 3: How thick are these coatings typically applied on speaker cabinets, and how much weight does the coating add?

### Takeaway
OEM polyurea finishes are about 1.5 mm (JBL states 0.060" for DuraFlex on WRC/WRX models); truck-bed liners are typically sprayed at 62–125 mils (1.6–3.2 mm); roll-on 1K PU liners build 0.7–1.0 mm in two coats; water-based texture paints build only a fraction of a millimetre (coverage ≈2.5 m²/kg at 40–55 % solids). Weight added ranges from well under 1 kg per cabinet for acrylic texture paint to several kg for a full-thickness polyurea skin **[ESTIMATE]**.

### Cited Findings
- JBL describes DuraFlex as a 0.060" (1.5 mm) coating on the exterior of its WRC/WRX weather-resistant AE/PD/VLA models — [JBL Standardized WR Configurations, AE/PD/VLA, Mar 2021](https://goknight.com/content/documentation/JBL_Standardized_WR-Configs_AE-PD-VLA_Series_Mar21%20(1).pdf); see also [JBL PD525S WR configurations](https://www.manualowl.com/m/JBL/PD525S/Manual/717560)
- "Most well-known spray-in bed liner companies typically apply their products between 62 and 125 mils" — bedliner trade article in the retrieved result set ([Off Road Xtreme, "Understanding Bedliners Better"](https://www.offroadxtreme.com/?p=103495); also syndicated at [Diesel Army](https://www.dieselarmy.com/?p=8520)) — attribution taken from the search extract; page not opened.
- Durabak: wet film 17–25 mils per coat, total dry film 29–39 mils for two coats — [Durabak Indoor TDS](https://www.durabakcompany.com/pages/durabak-indoor-technical-data-sheet)
- Tuff Cab coverage: 2–3 m²/kg (rough/textured), 4–6 m²/kg (smooth) — [Blue Aran blog](https://blog.bluearan.co.uk/?p=88)
- Warnex Strukturlack 1K: solids 54 wt%, wet density 1.232 g/cm³ — [Warnex SDS](https://adamhall.s3.amazonaws.com/media/MARKEN/WARNEX/0131WHI/0131WHI_WARNEX%20Strukturlack_Technisches_Datenblatt_de.pdf)

- Powder coating typically adds 1.5–6 mils (38–150 µm) — [Astrocorp sheet metal powder coating guide](https://astrocorp-sheetmetal.pages.dev/en/blog/sheet-metal-powder-coating-techniques-and-applications); PPG epoxy-polyester recommends 2–4 mils — [PPG PCFB90105](https://buyat.ppg.com/rep_powder/PDFs/CDPFILES/PCFB90105.pdf)
- A Line-X-coated bass cab is "much thicker and more resilient than the Duratex that I applied at home" — [TalkBass](https://www.talkbass.com/threads/coating-a-bass-cab-in-line-x.1265520/)

### Inferences
- **[ESTIMATE] Water-based texture paint mass:** at 2.5 m²/kg wet (Tuff Cab rough figure) and ~45–55 % solids, dry deposit ≈ 0.18–0.22 kg/m². A typical 12"/2-way cabinet (~1.5–2.0 m² external area) therefore gains ≈ 0.3–0.45 kg; a double-18 sub (~4 m²) ≈ 0.7–0.9 kg. Equivalent dry film at 1.2–1.5 g/cm³ dry density ≈ 120–180 µm average, with texture peaks higher.
- **[ESTIMATE] Hot-spray polyurea at 1.5 mm (JBL DuraFlex figure):** assuming an elastomer density of ~1.0–1.1 g/cm³ (not sourced in this session), the skin weighs ≈ 1.5–1.65 kg/m², i.e. ≈ 2.5–3.3 kg on a 2 m² cabinet and ≈ 6–6.5 kg on a 4 m² subwoofer. This is the main reason OEMs with polyurea finishes quote it only on specific series and why DIY builders report Line-X cabs as noticeably heavier/thicker.
- **[ESTIMATE] Roll-on Durabak at 29–39 mils (0.74–1.0 mm):** ≈ 0.8–1.1 kg/m² dry → ≈ 1.6–2.2 kg on a 2 m² cabinet.

### Gaps
- No manufacturer publishes a per-cabinet weight for polyurea finishes; no speaker-specific dry-film thickness for Warnex/Tuff Cab/Duratex was found (only coverage rates).
- Polyurea cured density was not retrieved from a TDS in this session; the 1.0–1.1 g/cm³ assumption should be checked against the Rhino/Line-X sheets (which normally list specific gravity).
- d&b does not publish PCP thickness in the retrieved spec texts.

## Key question 4: What surface prep is needed (sanding grit, edge rounding, sealing plywood voids, primer, denib, texture coat, top coat) and what application equipment is used?

### Takeaway
Water-based speaker paints go straight onto sanded bare plywood/MDF with no primer (Tuff Cab, Duratex), applied by honeycomb/texture roller or hopper gun; Warnex is viscous (900–1400 mPa·s) and is factory-sprayed from a pressure pot in a closed system, optionally oven-dried at ~60 °C. Hot-spray polyurea requires a heated plural-component rig and franchise/industrial facilities; DIY 1K/2K liners are rolled or sprayed with a Schutz-type gun. Community practice for wood: round edges (¾" radius cited as a good target), seal end grain/voids with thinned PVA or filler primer, sand 80→220 (no finer than ~320 so the coating can key), then texture coats.

### Cited Findings
**Water-based texture paints**
- Tuff Cab: "designed for optimum adhesion to bare timber… No primer is required before applying the Tuff Cab", provided there is no oil/grease; apply with a honeycomb roller for texture (a 4" roller suits small jobs); apply at room temperature — below 12 °C drying slows considerably, above ~25 °C the surface can dry too fast; store ≥5 °C and preferably ≤25 °C; shelf life 6 months, extendable to ~18 months at room temperature — [Blue Aran blog](https://blog.bluearan.co.uk/?p=88); [Blue Aran blog archive](https://blog.bluearan.co.uk/page/22/)
- Tuff Cab "is intended for bare wood and doesn't always stick to a previous coating particularly well" — [Blue Room forum](https://www.blue-room.org.uk/topic/71332-painting-speakers/)
- Warnex: the 1K texture paint (Series 74) "has a high viscosity demanding the use of a paint pressure tank. Because of the fast initial drying, a closed system is required"; "Coated substrates may be heated at elevated temperatures of approx. 60°C to speed up drying process"; store frost-free, max 12 months unopened; pre-spray WARNEX Hydrogrund for optimal adhesion (brochure context is plastics and "other materials") — [Warnex brochure](https://en.toutlehautparleur.com/media/catalog/product/datasheet/warnecke/WARNEX.pdf); Warnex SDS: store 15–30 °C; wear EN 374 gloves >0.4 mm, tight goggles when spraying; use respirator if solvent concentration exceeds workplace limits — [Warnex SDS](https://adamhall.s3.amazonaws.com/media/MARKEN/WARNEX/0131WHI/0131WHI_WARNEX%20Strukturlack_Technisches_Datenblatt_de.pdf)
- DuraTex: intended for wood, metal and fiberglass; for metal Acry-Tech recommends a water-based primer such as Zinsser 123; adheres to painted surfaces "provided the surfaces are sanded"; do not dilute Roller Grade ("it will change the chemical composition"), use Spray Grade if a thinner product is needed — [Parts Express FAQ, DuraTex Roller Grade 1 gal](https://www.parts-express.com/Acry-Tech-DuraTex-Black-1-Gal-Roller-Grade-Cabinet-Coating-260-101); [SoundImports DuraTex FAQ](https://soundimports.eu/en/go/product/82517983)
- Acry-Tech application tips: keep the work area at or above 65 °F; in high humidity or cold it may take 2–3 h to dry; once dry to the touch it has reached hard cure; no sanding between coats; when using a hopper gun, spray in a drop-cloth-covered area, keep the coat uniform, clean hopper and air cap immediately — [Acry-Tech "Applying DuraTex Tips & Tricks"](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/1639/9445/files/Applying-DuraTex-Tips-Tricks-1.pdf)
- DIY technique: apply the first Duratex coat only around seams, block-sand, then full coats — [Midwest Audio Club DIY forum](https://diy.midwestaudio.club/discussion/comment/34486/); one user found Duratex harder to sand than epoxy — [DIYAudio post](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/goto/post?id=2773368)
- Hopper-gun hardware example: Graco TexSpray FastFinish ships with 4, 6 and 8 mm nozzles — [Graco TexSpray FastFinish](https://graco.com/us/en/contractor/product/25d496-texspray-fastfinish.html)

**Hot-spray and DIY liners**
- Spray-on lining of polyurethane/polyurea with isocyanates is covered by an industry guidance document (equipment, ventilation, PPE) — [ACC/CPI Guidance Document on Spray-on Polyurethane/Polyurea Based Lining Applications](https://www.americanchemistry.com/content/download/4854/file/Guidance-Document-on-Spray-on-Polyurethane-Polyurea-Based-Lining-Applications-Containing-Isocyanates.pdf)
- Raptor: 3:1 mix, 60-min pot life at 20 °C, applied by Schutz/HVLP gun or aerosol — [U-POL Raptor TDS](https://u-pol.com/wp-content/uploads/RLBAL-TDS-EN.pdf); [U-POL Raptor commercial brochure](https://u-pol.com/wp-content/uploads/21336_Upol_Raptor_Commercial_Design_16pp.pdf)
- Durabak: two roller coats, 17–25 mils wet each — [Durabak Indoor TDS](https://www.durabakcompany.com/pages/durabak-indoor-technical-data-sheet)

**Wood prep (community practice)**
- "Testing of perceived tweeter harshness suggested ¾ inch roundover was the minimum"; veneer will bend around a ¾" routed MDF edge — [DIYAudio, veneering rounded-over speaker box](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/veneering-rounded-over-speaker-box.98613)
- Seal roundovers/end grain: "Coat the baffle edges (the roundover) with PVA glue and let it dry. Do a couple coats and sand"; another builder used three coats of 50/50 PVA/water, 20–30 min between coats, light 320-grit sand — [DIYAudio Zaph SR-71 build](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/pdx-zaph-sr-71-build-thread.120330/page-5); [DIYAudio, finishing options for MDF](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/finishing-options-for-mdf.151944/)
- Grit progression: start 80, work to at least 220 on a block; or 240 then 360/400 — "You do not need to go any finer… subsequent coats of paint need to 'key' into the surface"; "A proper wood primer must be used as automotive primer is too thin… exposed MDF will likely need a rub down and further priming" — [DIYAudio, smooth finish on round enclosure](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/how-to-achieve-a-smooth-finish-on-a-round-enclosure.239978)
- Filler primer: "2 wet coats w/ the filler primer and it's all gone! Hand sanded w/ some 400 grit and one more coat"; cabinet makers "almost unanimously recommended Shellac primer"; conflicting advice on whether to fill voids before or after priming — [Audioholics ER18 build](https://forums.audioholics.com/forums/threads/my-er18-build.78329/post-862352); [Audioholics post](https://forums.audioholics.com/forums/goto/post?id=868892); [DIYAudio Gedlee Summa build](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/matts-gedlee-summa-abbey-kit-build.136239/page-6)
- Epoxy fillets/epoxy-coated plywood cabinets as a sealing approach — [DIYAudio, speaker cabinets with epoxy and fillets](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/speaker-cabinets-with-epoxy-and-fillets.433278/)

### Inferences
- Edge radius matters for every coating family but for different reasons: thin acrylic films thin out and chip first on sharp arrises; thick elastomers bridge poorly over sharp corners and are the first place peel initiates; a ≥6–10 mm (¼–⅜") roundover is the practical minimum for texture paint and larger for polyurea (OEM polyurea cabinets typically have generously radiused edges) — the ¾" figure in the forum is an acoustic diffraction recommendation, not a coating one.
- Because Warnex needs a pressure pot and closed system, it is effectively a factory/finishing-shop product in Europe, whereas Tuff Cab/Duratex are explicitly roller-friendly for DIY — this maps directly onto who uses what (Q6).
- "No sanding between coats" (Duratex) and "sticks poorly over old paint" (Tuff Cab) both reflect coalescing acrylic films that bond to themselves while wet-on-dry but need a keyed, porous substrate for first-coat adhesion.

### Gaps
- No manufacturer document specifying sanding grit, edge radius, or void-filling for Tuff Cab/Warnex/Duratex was retrieved; all prep guidance is community practice.
- Plural-component rig parameters (material temperature, pressure, mix-ratio tolerances) for Line-X/Rhino could not be read from TDS in this session.
- Warnex spray parameters (nozzle, pressure, pot pressure, recommended wet film) are in the manufacturer TDS that could not be opened.

## Key question 5: Which coatings can be colour-matched to RAL and which are black-only? Do they fade/chalk in UV?

### Takeaway
Water-based texture paints (Warnex, Tuff Cab, Duratex) are tintable/offered in colours (Warnex SDS itself is for RAL 9010 pure white), and d&b offers its polyurea cabinets in custom RAL over a standard RAL 9005 top coat; Line-X Xtra is marketed as tintable to any automotive colour. ## Key question 6: Which coatings are used by commercial pro-audio OEMs vs. DIY builders, and why?

### Takeaway
Large touring-grade OEMs use in-house or contracted hot-spray polyurea systems (d&b "PCP – Polyurea Cabinet Protection"; JBL "DuraFlex", a multi-layer finish with an aliphatic top layer, 1.5 mm on WR models), because plural-component spraying needs isocyanate-rated booths, supplied-air PPE and heated rigs that only make sense at factory throughput. Smaller manufacturers and the entire DIY/rental-repair scene use 1K water-based texture paints (Warnex in continental Europe; Tuff Cab in the UK; Duratex in North America) because they need no primer, no isocyanate controls, roll or hopper-gun application and easy touch-up; Line-X/Rhino franchise shops are used by individual DIYers for one-off cabs at bed-liner pricing.

## Key question 7: What are known failure modes (peeling at edges, chipping at corners, greying/scuffing, solvent softening, water ingress) and how repairable is each system?

### Takeaway
Reported weaknesses: thin acrylic texture paints scuff grey and wear through at corners but are trivially re-rolled; they adhere poorly over old paint (Tuff Cab) and need bare, sanded wood. Polyurea skins almost never wear through but are rough, trap dirt, are hard to clean, cannot be blended invisibly without the original rig, and once chipped at a corner can let water into plywood; aromatic grades chalk outdoors. Epoxy on wood cracks; 1K moisture-cure PU (Durabak indoor) is not UV-stable.

### Cited Findings
- Line-X-style coatings "hold dirt well, and are difficult to get really clean" — [SoundForums official cabinet finishing thread](https://www.soundforums.net/community/threads/the-official-cabinet-finishing-thread.1015/page-2)
- Line-X is "harder and less flexible and is very rough to the touch" (vs Duratex) — [DIYAudio](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/duratex-or-other-options.419572/)
- Tuff Cab "doesn't always stick to a previous coating particularly well" — [Blue Room forum](https://www.blue-room.org.uk/topic/71332-painting-speakers/)
- Warnex is "easy to patch up down the road" — [SoundForums](https://www.soundforums.net/community/threads/the-official-cabinet-finishing-thread.1015/page-2)
- JBL: epoxy paints "can crack on wooden cabinets"; JBL offers a DuraFlex touch-up kit (part 30052) for damaged finishes — [JBL AC26 guide](https://www.manualowl.com/m/JBL/AC26/Manual/717474?page=30); [Harman DuraFlex repair kit](https://harman.helpjuice.com/general-jbl-inquiries/duraflex-repair-kit)
- Aromatic liners fade/oxidise in UV — [BodyShop Business](https://www.bodyshopbusiness.com/tough-and-durable-sprayed-on-bedliner/)
- Durabak indoor grade is "not UV stable" — [Durabak Indoor TDS](https://www.durabakcompany.com/pages/durabak-indoor-technical-data-sheet)
- MDF cabinets "tend to deteriorate, with water ingress as a likely cause"; plywood recommended — [bike-power.co.uk speaker repair blog](https://bike-power.co.uk/2015/06/speaker-repair/)
- Water-damaged cabinets can look fine outside while the panel is "wavy under the fingers"; rot found only when paint went through into soft wood; water-damaged corners often need wood splicing — [Audiokarma water-damaged KLH 17](https://audiokarma.org/forums/threads/water-damaged-klh-17-cabinet.759130/); [Classic Speaker Pages cabinet repair](https://community.classicspeakerpages.net/topic/2789-cabinet-repair/)
- Duratex: adheres to painted surfaces only if sanded; no sanding needed between its own coats (self-recoatable) — [Parts Express FAQ](https://www.parts-express.com/Acry-Tech-DuraTex-Black-1-Gal-Roller-Grade-Cabinet-Coating-260-101); [Acry-Tech tips](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/1639/9445/files/Applying-DuraTex-Tips-Tricks-1.pdf)

### Inferences
- Repairability ranking (best to worst): water-based texture paints (re-roll a panel, texture hides the blend) > roll-on 1K/2K liners (Raptor/Durabak can be re-rolled but texture/gloss mismatch is visible) > hot-spray polyurea (needs either the OEM touch-up kit or a brushable polyurea/urethane patch; a true blend requires re-spraying the whole panel). This is consistent with the forum statements and with JBL selling a dedicated repair kit.
- Edge-peel in elastomers is a stress-concentration problem: a thick, strong film under tension at a sharp arris will lift from the first chip; adequate roundover and a primer/sealer on end grain are the mitigations OEMs build in.
- Water ingress is more dangerous under a polyurea skin than under acrylic paint because the skin is near-impermeable: moisture that enters through a corner chip cannot dry back out through the coating, promoting plywood delamination under an apparently intact finish (mechanism inferred from the water-damage reports; not directly documented for polyurea cabinets).
- Solvent softening: cured polyurea/PU are highly solvent-resistant; water-based acrylics are susceptible to strong solvents and some cleaners — no speaker-specific source found, so treated as general coating knowledge (unsourced).

### Gaps
- No documented case study of polyurea peeling from a plywood cabinet edge was retrieved; the failure mode is reported anecdotally in forums that could not be opened in this session (TalkBass, SoundForums, Speakerplans).
- No chemical-resistance tables (ASTM D1308/D543) for any of the speaker paints were found.
- Greying/"burnishing" of black texture paints under abrasion is widely discussed on forums but no quotable statement was captured in the extracts.

## Key question 8: Health, safety, VOC and regulatory considerations

### Takeaway
All polyurea/polyurethane/polyaspartic systems (hot-spray and DIY Raptor/Durabak/Herculiner) contain isocyanates (MDI in liners, IPDI/HDI-type aliphatics in UV grades); spraying them requires supplied-air respirators, booths and training because isocyanates are potent respiratory sensitisers with very low exposure limits. Water-based texture paints avoid isocyanates but are not hazard-free: Warnex's SDS lists 6–9 % glycol ethers and isothiazolinone preservatives (skin sensitiser H317).

### Cited Findings
- Spraying isocyanate coatings requires a supplied-air respirator; an air-purifying respirator is acceptable only for rolling or brushing — [3M isocyanates technical bulletin 2024](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/2516912O/hmt-anz-isocyanates-technical-bulletin-2024.pdf)
- Options listed by industry guidance: full-facepiece organic-vapour/particulate respirator or supplied-air hood/helmet; isocyanates are sensitisers, so skin contact with spray mist must also be avoided — [CoatingsPro, "How to use polyurethane coatings safely"](https://coatingspromag.com/articles/safety/2016/09/how-to-use-polyurethane-coatings-safely)
- US respirator use falls under 29 CFR 1910.134 (written programme, fit testing); full-body coveralls, spray hood, goggles/full facepiece — [Professional Roofing, "Spray safe"](https://professionalroofing.net/articles/article/4378)
- OSHA ceiling for MDI commonly cited as 0.02 ppm (0.2 mg/m³); NIOSH figure 0.2 mg/m³ applied in a spray-on truck-bed-liner context; MDI exposure linked to occupational asthma and deaths — [IAM, "Preventing asthma and death from MDI exposure"](https://www.goiam.org/news/departments/preventing-asthma-and-death-from-mdi-exposure/)
- California CDPH HESIS isocyanate guidance: ventilation first; respirators for poorly ventilated spaces or heated/sprayed material; occupational asthma is a leading work-related lung disease — [CDPH HESIS isocyanates](https://Www.Cdph.Ca.gov/Programs/CCDPHP/DEODC/OHB/HESIS/CDPH%20Document%20Library/iso.pdf)
- ACC Center for the Polyurethanes Industry guidance document specific to spray-on polyurethane/polyurea lining applications — [ACC guidance PDF](https://www.americanchemistry.com/content/download/4854/file/Guidance-Document-on-Spray-on-Polyurethane-Polyurea-Based-Lining-Applications-Containing-Isocyanates.pdf); [CPI workplace health & safety](https://americanchemistry.com/industry-groups/center-for-the-polyurethanes-industry-cpi/workplace-health-and-safety); NIOSH isocyanates topic page (archived) — [NIOSH isocyanates](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20090831130218/http://www.cdc.gov/niosh/topics/isocyanates)

- DuraTex retailers describe it as low-VOC and non-toxic (marketing claims; SDS available) — [DuraTex SDS](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/1639/9445/files/DuraTex-Roller-Grade-DuraTex-Spray-Grade-SDS.pdf); [Parts Express listing](https://parts-express.com/Acry-Tech-DuraTex-Black-5-Gal-Spray-Grade-Cabinet-Coating-260-106)

### Inferences
- For a workshop deciding between routes, the regulatory step-change is isocyanate spraying, not VOC: 1K waterborne paints fall under ordinary paint-shop ventilation rules, whereas any sprayed 2K PU/polyurea triggers respirator programmes, booth requirements and (in the EU) mandatory diisocyanate training.
- Low-VOC "2.6 VOC" or 250 g/L liner variants show that DIY 2K liners are formulated to US regional VOC caps; waterborne texture paints are already far below typical limits.

- Specific VOC g/L for Warnex, Tuff Cab and Duratex were not found in the extracts.

## Key question 9: Other durable options — powder coating for grilles/hardware, hammertone, HPL/laminate, carpet, Tolex, Duraflex

### Takeaway
Steel grilles and aluminium hardware are powder-coated (epoxy-polyester hybrid or polyester, 50–100 µm, ~2H pencil hardness); soft coverings (carpet/Tolex) and HPL are alternatives for lower-abuse or furniture-style cabinets. Only sparse, non-speaker-specific data was retrievable for these in this session.

### Cited Findings
- PPG epoxy-polyester hybrid powder: 2.0–4.0 mils (≈51–102 µm) film, ASTM D7091 thickness, 2H pencil hardness (ASTM D3363) — [PPG PCFB90105](https://buyat.ppg.com/rep_powder/PDFs/CDPFILES/PCFB90105.pdf)
- Powder coat typical film 1.5–6 mils (38–150 µm); polyester for UV resistance, epoxy for chemical/corrosion resistance — [Astrocorp](https://astrocorp-sheetmetal.pages.dev/en/blog/sheet-metal-powder-coating-techniques-and-applications); [Fecision](https://fecision.com/?p=12773); enclosure coating comparison listing 2H–3H pencil ranges — [Saginaw Control coating comparison](https://saginawcontrol.com/instman/124.PDF)
- Community coverage-preference discussion (carpet vs Tolex vs paint) — [TalkBass, "What cab covering material do you prefer?"](https://www.talkbass.com/threads/what-cab-covering-material-do-you-prefer.346446/); Tolex re-covering thread — [TalkBass, recovering cabinet Tolex](https://www.talkbass.com/threads/recovering-cabinet-tolex.863075/); HPL/laminate on cabinets — [DIYAudio, laminating cabinets](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/laminating-cabinets.76281/page-4)
- JBL's WR configurations specify extra treatments beyond DuraFlex for weather-resistant models (details in the PDF, which could not be opened) — [JBL WR configs](https://goknight.com/content/documentation/JBL_Standardized_WR-Configs_AE-PD-VLA_Series_Mar21%20(1).pdf)
- Hammertone/"Hammerite-style" finishes, Herculiner, ArmorThane, Speedliner, Bullet Liner: no data retrieved (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- Powder coat is the natural fit for grilles because the substrate is metal and can be oven-cured at 180–200 °C; it is irrelevant for the wood box itself (wood cannot take the cure temperature without special low-temp powders).
- Carpet and Tolex protect by sacrificial compliance (they tear rather than chip) and are cheap to re-cover, but they absorb water and dirt, which is why touring pro audio moved to sprayed/rolled textured coatings.

### Gaps
- No speaker-grille-specific powder-coat spec (steel gauge, perforation, film target) was found.
- Hammerite/hammertone alkyd data, HPL abrasion (EN 438) values, Tolex/vinyl specs: not researched before the search budget ran out.
