# Boutique / sound-system / DIY practice for durable, scratch-resistant speaker-cabinet finishes

Research note on how small pro-audio builders, UK/EU sound-system crews, German/Russian DIY communities and US DIY forums finish cabinets: products, recipes, failure modes, repairs, cost, regional equivalents and how factory finishes are perceived.

**Method / limitations (read first).** In this session every direct page fetch was blocked (DNS for all external hosts refused), and the shared web-search budget ran out after ~55 searches. All findings below therefore come from search-engine result snippets and result summaries of the cited pages, not from full reads of the threads. Quotes are as returned by the search tool; usernames and post dates were mostly not exposed, so they are given only where the snippet carried them. Forum material is anecdotal unless explicitly labelled as a manufacturer datasheet. Several items the brief asked for (YouTube URLs, BASSBOSS/Danley/Funktion-One factory process, Penn Elcom "Pro-Coat", Italian equivalents, Speakerplans designer-specific finishes) could not be confirmed and are logged as gaps.

## KQ1. Which textured speaker paints do experienced builders rate highest for scratch resistance, and why? Are there head-to-head tests?

### Takeaway
Community consensus (UK Speakerplans, DE lautsprecherforum/paforum, RU show-master) is a clear ladder: 1K water-based texture paints (Tuff Cab < Warnex ≈ HIFIDECO/Armacab/Duratex) are convenient and repairable but "not touring grade"; 2K polyurethane (Trimite-type, U-Pol Raptor) is noticeably harder; hot-spray polyurea/PU hybrids (Line-X, Rhino, Penn Guard) are the top of the durability ladder but require professional application. No independent head-to-head abrasion test of these products was found; all rankings are user opinion plus manufacturer data.

### Cited Findings
- Warnex and Tuff Cab are both water-based acrylic emulsions "slightly different in composition"; several Speakerplans members say Warnex is harder-wearing ("does seem to be harder wearing", "surprisingly durable for a single pack paint") and "lends itself to spraying better than TuffCab does"; Warnex is "virtually completely matt", standard Tuff Cab is glossy, Tuff Cab Pro is satin and closer to Warnex — [Speakerplans: Warnex vs Tuff Cab](https://forum.speakerplans.com/warnex-vs-tuff-cab_topic51404.html); [Speakerplans: Tuff Cab, Warnex or Trimite?](https://forum.speakerplans.com/tuff-cab-warnex-or-trimite_topic47199.html); [Speakerplans: Painting the boxes](https://forum.speakerplans.com/painting-the-boxes_topic95465.html)
- Tuff Cab's own maker is quoted on Speakerplans as saying "Tuff Cab is not quite touring grade paint"; its selling points are "easy to apply, no nasty fumes, fairly cheap and really quick to touch up cabs" — [Speakerplans: Heavy duty paint rather than tuff cab](https://forum.speakerplans.com/heavy-duty-paint-rather-than-tuff-cab_topic107268.html); [Speakerplans: SPEAKER PAINT p.2](https://forum.speakerplans.com/speaker-paint_topic92253_page2.html)

### Inferences
- The durability ranking that recurs across four language communities is: polyurea/PU hot-spray > 2K PU (Raptor, Trimite) > Warnex ≈ HIFIDECO ≈ Duratex (Pro-grade 1K) > Tuff Cab/Aldcroft (budget 1K). Rankings are opinion-based; the only quantitative data are manufacturer film-thickness/coverage figures.
- "Scratch resistance" in user reports is dominated by film build and hardness; 1K paints win on repairability, not on hardness.

### Gaps
- No independent, documented abrasion/scratch (Taber, pencil hardness, falling sand) comparison of Warnex vs Tuff Cab vs Duratex vs Armacab vs Line-X was found.
- Penn Elcom "Pro-Coat" could not be located; Penn Elcom's current textured products are Armacab (1K water-based) and Penn Guard (polyurea spray) — see KQ7. "Hybrid Coatings" brand not found in results.

## KQ2. Recommended prep and application recipe (coats, roller type, dilution, drying, sealing)

### Takeaway
For 1K texture paints the field recipe is: bare, degreased, P80–P120-sanded timber; seal only fillers/screw-holes (thin PVA) or use a 50:50 water-thinned first coat; no primer on ply/MDF; 2–4 coats with a "honeycomb"/coarse foam texture roller at 12–25 °C; build thick (≈2.5 kg per 12" top according to one Speakerplans user; Warnex datasheet 70–120 µm DFT); allow days to weeks to reach full hardness. Warnex is designed for pressure-pot spraying (1.5–2 mm nozzle, 30–40 cm) and most German users say do not thin it.

### Cited Findings
**Tuff Cab (UK, Blue Aran/TuffCab Ltd)**
- Blue Aran guide: bonds best to bare timber, remove oil/grease, paint directly on bare wood without primer; optional 50:50 water-diluted base coat; any cellulose-based paint must be fully removed; suits porous materials (MDF, natural wood), not ABS/polypropylene; paint at room temp — below 12 °C drying slows considerably, above 25 °C surface dries too fast; roller is the recommended method, honeycomb roller gives the texture, 4-inch roller for small jobs; two to three coats usually enough; first coat dries 30–60 min (2–4 h in a cool room); wrap roller in plastic between coats; shelf life 6 months, up to 18 months at room temperature — [Blue Aran blog: Tuff Cab Loudspeaker Paint](https://blog.bluearan.co.uk/?p=88) (also at [this URL](https://blog.bluearan.co.uk/tuff-cab-loudspeaker-paint/))
- TuffCab.com hints: bare wood works best, no primer needed for darker colours, lighter colours benefit from a white primer; solvent-based filler should be sealed with PVA first; belt sander with P80 or P120 is typical and scratches are hidden because the paint is very viscous; coverage "rough finish 2-3 m² per kg, smooth finish 4-6 m² per kg"; use a short-pile roller when touching up a cabinet that already has enough texture; "TuffCab wont bond well to Polyurea paints, or other paints which are solvented" — [TuffCab hints 1: Surface preparation](https://tuffcab.com/hints.php?infopage=1); [TuffCab hints 2: Ready to start painting?](https://tuffcab.com/hints.php?infopage=2)
- Speakerplans user recipes: sand for a key, light PVA coat over filled screw holes, Tuff Cab straight onto ply with no primer, "allow a couple of weeks to fully harden"; "two coats with a brush and then two with a textured roller"; "3-4 thin coats, then 2 thick textured coats"; to spray with a cheap gun one user had to add ~25 % water and drying then took a long time; scoops left touching overnight "stuck together into a single block" — [Speakerplans: Painting my cabs (54737)](https://forum.speakerplans.com/painting-my-cabs_topic54737.html); [Speakerplans: Painting my cabs (76852)](https://forum.speakerplans.com/painting-my-cabs_topic76852.html); [Speakerplans: painting with tuff cab](https://forum.speakerplans.com/painting-with-tuff-cab_topic51547.html); [Speakerplans: Speaker cab paint](https://forum.speakerplans.com/speaker-cab-paint_topic48310.html)
- Speakerplans warns Tuff Cab over polyurethane "will never bond properly and will gradually flake off"; sand back to bare wood if the old finish is 2-pack polyester or cellulose — [Speakerplans: Tuff-Cab alternative?](https://forum.speakerplans.com/tuffcab-alturnative_topic91956.html)

**Warnex (DE, Warnecke & Böhm / WB Coatings)**
- Technical datasheet WB74-1: application by high-pressure spraying with pressure vessel or pressure-cup gun; nozzle 1.5–2.0 mm; spray distance 30–40 cm; thinner = deionised water; recommended dry film 70 µm single coat ("Narbenstruktur") or 120 µm in two coats ("Tüpfelstruktur"); dust-dry after 15–20 min, assembly-firm after 2–3 h, through-dried after 4 days (20 °C / 65 % RH / 70 µm) — [Warnex TDS WB74-1 (powerpreis24)](https://powerpreis24.de/mediafiles/Datenblaetter/warneke/TDS_WB_74-1_WARNEX_Strukturlack_1K_DE-EN.pdf); [Warnex Technisches Merkblatt (aweo)](https://www.aweo.de/mediafiles/PDF/WARNEX_Technisches_Merkblatt.pdf); [same (hs-sound)](https://www.hs-sound.de/mediafiles/Sonstiges/warnex_datenblatt.pdf)
- Warnex brochure (Thomann-hosted): for plastics pre-spray with WARNEX Hydrogrund (series 72, air-drying); no primer needed on MDF or birch plywood and no intermediate sanding necessary — [Warnex Broschüre & Verarbeitungshinweise](https://images.thomann.de/pics/prod/208234_datenblatt.pdf)
- French distributor datasheet/listing: ~120 µm total build; "high viscosity, so it calls for a pressure tank"; coverage estimate ~1.5 m² per kg depending on method; sold in RAL 7016 grey, RAL 9010 white and black; marketed for "honeycomb roller application" — [toutlehautparleur WARNEX datasheet](https://www.toutlehautparleur.com/media/catalog/product/datasheet/warnecke/WARNEX.pdf); [toutlehautparleur Warnex RAL 7016 1 kg](https://en.toutlehautparleur.com/warnex-1kg-professional-paint-pot-grey-ral-7016-textured-special-for-enclosures-honeycomb-roller-application.html)
- Retailer text: "For optimal results use a Spray Gun with pressure cup. For repairs and touch-ups, the paint can also be applied with a roller or a brush"; protect from frost, heat and direct sun — [Digitec WB Coatings 0131](https://www.digitec.ch/de/s1/product/wb-coatings-0131-whi-strukturlack-weiss-lautsprecher-bauteile-17521719); [Adam Hall 013112](https://www.adamhall.com/shop/en/brands/013112-013112)

- Speakerplans Warnex roller practice: water-thinning disputed ("can be painted right out of the tin. **Duratex (US, Acry-Tech)**
- Manufacturer tips: work in good light to see texture variation; suspend the cabinet face down over a support, coat bottom first then turn; remove (or mask) hardware; "DuraTex is self-priming and has great adhesion", no primer on bare wood; roller application needs "a lot of material to fully wet the surface"; test texture on scrap/cardboard first — [Acry-Tech: Applying DuraTex Tips & Tricks](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/1639/9445/files/Applying-DuraTex-Tips-Tricks-1.pdf); [Acry-Tech: Applying DuraTex](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/1639/9445/files/Applying-DuraTex.pdf)
- Forum practice: load roller from a paper plate and roll out on a second plate for even coats — [Midwest Audio Club](https://diy.midwestaudio.club/discussion/comment/9175/); smooth base coat with regular roller then splatter coat with textured roller — [Soundforums: official cabinet finishing thread](https://soundforums.net/community/goto/post?id=24319); one heavy coat to cover bare wood plus one light coat for voids "took most of a gallon" on two cabinets; stucco sleeves work but not as well as the Acry-Tech sleeve; a plain foam roller gives a finish "like having sand in the paint" — [diyaudio: Need cabs for two Kappa Pro LF2's p.6](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/need-cabs-for-two-emminence-kappapro-lf2-s.281633/page-6); [diyaudio: Duratex or other options](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/duratex-or-other-options.419572/)

**Penn Elcom Armacab (UK, 1K water-based)**
- Penn Elcom: a 4-inch coarse foam texture roller produces a raised, even texture close to spray spatter; applicable by pressure-assisted spray, roller or by hand; UV stable; coverage 4.6 m²/L at 200 µm, 2.3 m²/L at 400 µm — [Penn Elcom blog: What is Armacab?](https://www.penn-elcom.com/us/blog/product-spotlight-armacab-textured-speaker-cabinet-paint.html); [Armacab black 1 L](https://www.penn-elcom.com/armacab-black-textured-speaker-cabinet-paint-1-litre-armacab1lk); [Armacab black 5 L](https://www.penn-elcom.com/armacab-black-textured-speaker-cabinet-paint-5-litres-armacab5lk)

**Generic prep: edges, sealing, MDF**
- The Loudspeaker Kit finishing guide (textured black): sand MDF's waxy surface so texture paint adheres; fill/flatten uneven edges before routing; rounding edges "conceals butt joins that tend to crack paint finishes"; example radii 13 mm on cabinet edges, 6 mm front/back — [LSK DIY finishing guide – textured black (PDF)](https://www.theloudspeakerkit.com/assets/files/LSK%20DIY%20speaker%20finishing%20guide%20-%20textured%20black.pdf)

### Inferences
- Film build is the main lever for 1K paints: the two independent "it works" reports (2.5 kg per 12" top; 4 coats) and the two "it failed" categories (thin coats, cold application) point the same way.
- Speakerplans and TuffCab both favour PVA only on filler/holes rather than whole-cabinet PVA sealing; German sources favour no primer at all on ply/MDF. Whole-surface PVA sealing is common builder folklore but not recommended by any manufacturer found.

### Gaps
- No manufacturer gives a recommended roller nap/pore size beyond "honeycomb"/"coarse foam"; no source gave hopper-gun orifice/pressure settings for Duratex spray grade.
- Tuff Cab's exact dry-film or coats-to-thickness data not found.

## KQ3. When do builders go to polyurea / bed liner (Line-X, Rhino, Raptor) and what are the trade-offs?

### Takeaway
Builders escalate to sprayed polyurea/PU when cabinets are rental/touring stock or when they want a "factory" look; accepted costs are price (~US$200 per 215-size cab quoted), isocyanate hazard (air-fed respirator for Raptor; Line-X only by franchise), weight (~0.7 lb/ft² at 1/8", but cabinets get 2–3 mm), poor repairability/colour-matching, every surface flaw telegraphing through, and reports of liner delaminating as a "shell" if ply is not sealed/keyed.

### Cited Findings
- Line-X spec quoted: "0.7 pounds per square foot at one eighth of an inch thickness"; cabinets receive "maybe like 2 or 3 mm, surely not 7 to 9 mm"; weight estimate = exterior area × spec; one quote of ~$200 to coat a 215-size cabinet, "just too much for coating" — [TalkBass: Line-X coating – influence on the sound?](https://www.talkbass.com/threads/line-x-coating-influence-on-the-sound.1155389/); [TalkBass: Coating a bass cab in Line X](https://www.talkbass.com/threads/coating-a-bass-cab-in-line-x.1265520/)
- Prep for Line-X: sand for adhesion; "sealing the cabinet with a polyurethane 2 component paint before Line-X gives the best results"; opinion split on whether Line-X sticks to plastic cabs with rough sanding — [diyaudio: Duratex or other options](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/duratex-or-other-options.419572/); [Soundforums: Line-X on QSC K-series?](https://soundforums.net/community/threads/line-x-on-qsc-k-series.10606)
- Soundforums Line-X prep thread: bedliner "will show every imperfection"; "almost all imperfections do stay visible"; sceptic: "there's no magic, only hype"; Rust-Oleum truck bed liner and POR-15 results disappointing — [Soundforums: preparation for Line-X bedliner on my cabinets](https://soundforums.net/community/threads/preparation-for-line-x-bedliner-on-my-cabinets.13947/post-13947); [Soundforums: Refinishing speaker cabs](https://soundforums.net/community/goto/post?id=15953)
- Delamination report (second-hand): "the bed liner does not adhere to the cabinet, acts as kind of shell, and can start to separate and possibly rattle over time" — [diyaudio: Where to find generic polyurea coating (THAM15)](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/where-to-find-generic-polyurea-coating.386328/)
- Line-X is "a polyurethane finish, generally applied with a texture"; it covers anything attached to the wood so remove hardware and fully mask driver holes — [Harmony Central: Painting a speaker cabinet](https://www.harmonycentral.com/forums/topic/207915-painting-a-speaker-cabinet/); [Harmony Central: Bedliner as cabinet finish](https://www.harmonycentral.com/forums/topic/120671-bedliner-as-cabinet-finish/); chopped fibreglass "is not as hard a finish as Line-X, so the finish will show wear more" — [MusicPlayer forums: Truck bed liner for amp](https://forums.musicplayer.com/topic/135765-truck-bed-liner-for-amp-any-suggestions/)
- Raptor (U-Pol) is a 2K PU with an isocyanate hardener; U-POL TDS says the hardener must only be used with Raptor; an air-fed respirator is recommended, a standard cartridge respirator may not be enough — [U-POL Raptor TDS](https://u-pol.com/wp-content/uploads/UP4801-TDS.pdf); [BackroadsVanner Raptor video write-up](https://www.backroadsvanner.com/all-posts/video/painting-van-with-raptor-bedliner-chevy-peeling-paint/)

- Speakerplans: "Raptor is very good, very tough, but it wont work very well over Toughcab" — [Speakerplans: Heavy duty paint rather than tuff cab](https://forum.speakerplans.com/heavy-duty-paint-rather-than-tuff-cab_topic107268.html); TuffCab likewise will not bond to polyurea — [TuffCab hints 2](https://tuffcab.com/hints.php?infopage=2)
- Roll-on 1K liners (Durabak/Herculiner): adhere to wood with/without primer if not sap-fresh; xylene wipe improves adhesion; 3 coats with long-nap mini rollers; "any nailholes/screwholes/etc will show through"; rolling gives more control than spraying — [HomeRecording thread](https://homerecording.com/bbs/threads/truck-bed-liner-for-speaker-cab-finish.304415/); [TalkBass Herculiner](https://www.talkbass.com/threads/herculiner-coating-on-my-speaker-cabinet.1341793/); [TalkBass: truckbed liner on speaker cabs p.2](https://www.talkbass.com/threads/truckbed-liner-on-speaker-cabs.625826/page-2); [JeepForum Herculiner vs Durabak](https://www.jeepforum.com/threads/herculiner-vs-durabak.132056/)

- Penn Guard = Penn Elcom's polyurea spray coating for speaker cabinets, now in smaller packages — [LSi Online: Penn Guard now in smaller packages](https://lsionline.com/news/penn-guard-now-in-smaller-packages-n581k0)

### Inferences
- The trade-off builders actually make is repairability vs hardness: 1K paints can be spot-rolled by anyone; polyurea needs the same franchise gun to repair, so crews with many cabs often stay 1K and simply recoat.

### Gaps

## KQ4. How do small builders repair chips/scratches and blend texture so repairs are invisible?

### Takeaway
With 1K texture paints the standard fix is: clean, sand no finer than ~P80, re-roll the damaged patch with a short-pile or textured mini-roller (or stipple with a brush), feathering into the surrounding texture; 60 ml touch-up pots are sold. Manufacturers warn that sheen, colour and texture of a new batch may not match an aged cabinet, and that textured PU/polyurea factory finishes (EAW "roadcoat") are hard to blend; aerosol texture products exist (HIFIDECO 400 ml spray) but no documented aerosol-blend method was found.

### Cited Findings
- TuffCab: coating can be wiped clean, "minor damage is easy to touch up"; 60 ml pot sold "to touch up minor scuffs and scrapes"; "use a short pile roller when touching up a previously painted cabinet that already has a thick enough texture"; when matching older cabinets "allow for possible differences in colour, sheen and texture"; new formulation note: "TuffCab manufactured from September 2026 replaces previous versions" — [TuffCab product TUFFCAB5BK](https://tuffcab.com/products.php?prod_id=TUFFCAB5BK); [TuffCab hints 2](https://tuffcab.com/hints.php?infopage=2); [Tuff Cab touch-up 60 ml](https://www.layguitars.com/product/tuff-cab-speaker-touch-up-paint-black-60ml/)
- TuffCab adhesion caveat for repairs over unknown finishes: "it is possible for Tuff Cab to not bond perfectly with some existing paint finishes, and you may need to rub down the surface" — [TuffCab product TUFFCABPRO5](https://tuffcab.com/products.php?prod_id=TUFFCABPRO5)
- Warnex: "For touch ups it works well with a stipple application. To get the prickly, textured finish you need to work harder"; "For repair and touch-up work, the paint can also be applied with a roller or brush" — [Thomann Warnex reviews](https://thomann.co.uk/warnex_strukturlack_1kg_reviews.htm); [Elektroda (PL) thread](https://www.elektroda.pl/rtvforum/topic609135.html); [Adam Hall Warnex](https://www.adamhall.com/shop/en/brands/013112-013112)
- Duratex: "I did two coats and touched it up with a brush where necessary"; "easy to touch up" — [Parts Express Duratex roller grade](https://www.parts-express.com/Acry-Tech-DuraTex-Black-1-Gal-Roller-Grade-Cabinet-Coating-260-101); Durabak "can be reapplied to the abraded area" — [HomeRecording thread](https://homerecording.com/bbs/threads/truck-bed-liner-for-speaker-cab-finish.304415/)
- ProSoundWeb: textured coatings "can be re-created with a brush"; sand no finer than 80 grit, remove dust — [ProSoundWeb wedges thread](https://forums.prosoundweb.com/index.php?topic=174603.0)
- EAW: RSX boxes use proprietary "roadcoat", a textured polyurethane; "This will make it difficult to blend touch-up paint with the rest of the cabinet" — [EAW knowledge base: paint used for RSX](https://design.eaw.com/knowledge/paint-used-for-rsx-array-and-loudspeaker-products)
- BASSBOSS sells "Touch Up Paint: Speaker Cabinet Coating" for gig scratches — [BASSBOSS help: How do I touch up the paint](https://help.bassboss.com/portal/en/kb/articles/how-do-i-touch-up-the-paint-on-my-bassboss-gear)
- Speakerplans repaint workflow for old cabs: strip with blowtorch + belt sander + DA (liquid strippers "completely useless"); or rough up gloss with a coarse belt and apply several coats of Tuff Cab ("pretty robust"); if old paint is water-based just clean it, if solvented Tuff Cab "will flake off" — [Speakerplans: Re painting cabs](https://forum.speakerplans.com/re-painting-cabs_topic102998.html); [Speakerplans: Repainting cabinets – not Tuff Cab](https://forum.speakerplans.com/repainting-cabinets-not-tuff-cab_topic102883.html)
- paforum: a Warnex recoat without intermediate sanding sits "relativ lose"; Warnex "hält auf Spachtelmasse und Grundierung nicht, anderes hingegen schon" — [paforum Warnex thread](https://paforum.de/forum/index.php?thread%2F120839-warnex-die-uns%C3%A4glichegeschichte%2F=)
- Russian: HIFIDECO sells a 400 ml aerosol alongside CY-610 primer and CY-689 texture finish — [Audiosila: Краска для акустики](https://audiosila.com/category/kraska-dlja-akustiki/)
- Polk owner forum suggests clear touch-up sprays over scratches (home hi-fi, not textured pro paint) — [Polk forum: LSiM cabinet scratches](https://forum.polkaudio.com/discussion/comment/2721889)

### Inferences
- Invisible repair of 1K texture paint is realistic only when the same product/batch and same roller are used and the whole panel (not just the chip) is re-rolled; otherwise sheen/texture mismatch shows. For polyurea factory finishes, owners effectively cannot blend repairs.

### Gaps
- No step-by-step documented aerosol-texture blending method for pro cabinets (only existence of HIFIDECO spray).
- BASSBOSS KB article content (coating type, procedure) could not be read.

## KQ5. Which mistakes cause premature failure?

### Takeaway
Across all communities the repeat failures are: painting 1K water-based paint over solvented/PU/polyurea or glossy old paint (flakes off); too-thin coats; cold application or frozen stock; unsealed filler, glue squeeze-out or dust under Warnex; sharp 90° edges; unsealed MDF/ply edges; insufficient cure time before stacking; and bed liner over unsealed, unkeyed ply (shell delamination).

### Cited Findings
- Over wrong substrate: Tuff Cab over polyurethane "will never bond properly and will gradually flake off"; TuffCab won't bond to polyurea or solvented paints; cellulose must be fully removed — [Speakerplans Tuff-Cab alternative](https://forum.speakerplans.com/tuffcab-alturnative_topic91956.html); [TuffCab hints 2](https://tuffcab.com/hints.php?infopage=2); [Blue Aran guide](https://blog.bluearan.co.uk/?p=88)
- Warnex adhesion failures: "Warnex hält weder auf Grundierungen, noch auf Acryl"; problems over filler transitions and wood glue/wood dust; boxes looked bad after some years outdoors — [paforum Warnex thread](https://paforum.de/forum/index.php?thread%2F120839-warnex-die-uns%C3%A4glichegeschichte%2F=); [lautsprecherforum: Warnex auf Acryllack?](https://www.lautsprecherforum.eu/viewtopic.php?f=29&t=68)
- Thin coats: "thin light coats won't do the job" — [Speakerplans warnex threads](https://forum.speakerplans.com/warnex-paint_topic27214.html)
- Temperature: Tuff Cab below 12 °C dries very slowly, above 25 °C surface-skins — [Blue Aran guide](https://blog.bluearan.co.uk/?p=88); Warnex that has frozen "ist die Sache kaputt und wird es nie mehr gut halten"; stored unheated it "nicht mehr ganz so prickelnd hält" — [lautsprecherforum: Fragen zu Warnex](https://www.lautsprecherforum.eu/viewtopic.php?t=7953)
- Cure time: Tuff Cab scoops stacked overnight fused together; allow "a couple of weeks to fully harden"; Raptor ~7 days to full hardness; Warnex 4 days through-dry — [Speakerplans Painting my cabs](https://forum.speakerplans.com/painting-my-cabs_topic76852.html); [Speakerplans Heavy duty paint](https://forum.speakerplans.com/heavy-duty-paint-rather-than-tuff-cab_topic107268.html); [Warnex TDS](https://powerpreis24.de/mediafiles/Datenblaetter/warneke/TDS_WB_74-1_WARNEX_Strukturlack_1K_DE-EN.pdf)
- Edges: "the 90° edges were the most vulnerable… where paint seemed to chip most easily"; rounding conceals butt joins that crack paint — [AVSForum: Cabinet roundovers to protect edges](https://www.avsforum.com/threads/cabinet-roundovers-to-protect-edges-of-flatpack-cabinets.2994212/); [LSK finishing guide](https://www.theloudspeakerkit.com/assets/files/LSK%20DIY%20speaker%20finishing%20guide%20-%20textured%20black.pdf); plywood roundovers need void-free ply and a sharp bit, one member advises against rounding ply at all — [Techtalk: 1/2" roundover on oak plywood](https://techtalk.parts-express.com/forum/tech-talk-forum/43619-1-2-roundover-on-oak-plywood)
- Unsealed edges soak up paint; MDF's waxy skin must be sanded — [Techtalk fill gaps](https://techtalk.parts-express.com/forum/tech-talk-forum/56437-what-to-use-to-fill-in-gaps-on-a-speaker-cabinet-build); [LSK guide](https://www.theloudspeakerkit.com/assets/files/LSK%20DIY%20speaker%20finishing%20guide%20-%20textured%20black.pdf)
- Surface flaws telegraph through bed liner and Herculiner (screw/nail holes) — [Soundforums Line-X prep](https://soundforums.net/community/threads/preparation-for-line-x-bedliner-on-my-cabinets.13947/post-13947); [TalkBass Herculiner](https://www.talkbass.com/threads/herculiner-coating-on-my-speaker-cabinet.1341793/)
- Bed liner shell delamination over unsealed cabinets — [diyaudio generic polyurea thread](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/where-to-find-generic-polyurea-coating.386328/)
- Shelf life/stock: Tuff Cab 6–18 months — [Blue Aran](https://blog.bluearan.co.uk/?p=88); Warnex nominally 1 year but users report 2–6 years if sealed and frost-free — [lautsprecherforum Fragen zu Warnex](https://www.lautsprecherforum.eu/viewtopic.php?t=7953)
- Spraying 1K paint diluted 25 % for a cheap gun prolongs drying and (implicitly) reduces build — [Speakerplans Painting my cabs](https://forum.speakerplans.com/painting-my-cabs_topic76852.html)
- Hardware: metal corners often do not fit a router-rounded edge; deep-insert corners can split thin panels — [TDPRI: Metal corners are a bad fit](https://www.tdpri.com/threads/metal-corners-are-a-bad-fit.876187/); corners + rubber feet treated as complementary protection — [Acoustic Guitar Forum: speaker cabinet corners](https://www.acousticguitarforum.com/forums/showthread.php?t=491888); range of corners — [Penn Elcom speaker corners](https://www.penn-elcom.com/us/speaker-hardware/speaker-corners)

### Inferences
- Most "Warnex/Tuff Cab is rubbish" reports in the German and Russian threads coincide with one of these errors (acrylic/filler underneath, cold shop, thin coats), which is why experiences are so polarised.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies how much edge radius is needed for texture paint survival; the 6–13 mm LSK numbers and 3/8" (≈10 mm) US figure are the only concrete values.
- No direct discussion found of whether to fit metal/PU corners before or after painting.
